Who is Mahesh?

What has made a young boy ‘The Mahesh’ he is today?

They say life changes in an instant, but mine shifted while I was suspended in the quiet dark.

When I was in the 12th grade, I suffered from cerebral venous sinus thrombosis, blood clots in the brain that left me in a coma for a week. When I finally woke up to consciousness, my memory was gone. I couldn’t even form the most basic sounds, the simple words a baby utters: mama, papa.

While medicine pointed to the clots, I knew deep down that my body was speaking an unexpressed language. It was the culmination of an overwhelming accumulation of discrimination, injustice, poverty, social divide and loss. In the 9th grade, I lost my uncle in an accident. Within a year, in the 10th grade, I lost my grandfather. And I couldn’t cry. And I knew someone who lost both of her parents.

For a couple of years following, I found myself writing poetry about death, trying to make sense of the life that has an end to it. I had been a strong academic student, but life as I knew it had dissolved. The tables completely turned.

Though people look up at the constellations to figure out their destiny, I think every human carries a universe within themselves and my true turning point was completely internal. When I began my graduation, majoring in English literature, I opted for psychology as an optional subject. I was already deeply fascinated by literature, poetry, and the raw, untold stories of human lives. Because who truly cares about just a profession? Our personal lives and stories are where we are truly alive.

And then the most tragic of my life was losing my close friend. Which took years to process the grief.

Studying basic psychology opened a door, but stepping into the real professional world shattered any remaining illusions of simple answers. Working closely with numerous individuals, women and men carrying the crushing weight of traumatic childhoods, parental neglect, sexual abuses, bullying, body shame, lack of self esteem and feeling low about oneself, I realized how complex, invisible, and agonizing their realities and internal struggles were. I wanted to understand why they lived the lives they did. That realization drew me into a much deeper dive.

Someone’s pain turned into anger, someone helplessly shrinked while someone thrived and succeeded.

My journey into healing became structured and intentional and yet I felt that I just followed the plot - unconsciously.

It began with ‘Be There Certificate’ from an organization founded by Lady Gaga.

Then I completed specialised courses

  1. Art and Movement Therapy
  2. Diploma in Professional Counseling
  3. Internationally Accredited Trauma & Recovery Practitioner
  4. Certified Psychology & Body Language Profiler

And Finally, I let myself go all in the ‘Master of Arts in Counseling Psychology’.

Today, I stand here not just as a professionally knowledgeable, experienced counselor, but as someone who understands what it means to live a life full of twists and turns, and yet rebuild from zero and end up being a heroic, resilient and deeply empathetic human being. I help people through the power of authentic conversation and empathetic presence. Honestly, I believe medicines have their place, usually when emotional and mental distress has been carried so long that it manifests physically as a clinical emergency, but true healing often begins when someone is finally listened to, understood and accepted unconditionally.
